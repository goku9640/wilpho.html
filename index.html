RESPONSIBLE WILDLIFE CHECKLIST

A respectful field routine

⌖
Keep a safe distance

Use a longer lens or a hide. Never approach, corner or pursue an animal for a closer frame.

◌
Do not bait or disturb

Never lure wildlife, handle young animals, disturb nests or alter habitats for a photograph.

♧
Protect the habitat

Stay on permitted paths, follow local rules and leave no litter or trace.

◎
Watch for stress

If an animal changes behaviour, becomes alert or moves away, give it space and stop.

↗
Share locations carefully

Avoid posting exact locations of vulnerable species or sensitive nesting sites.

QUICK REFERENCE

Your camera language guide.

From aperture to zebra stripes, build confidence with the terms you will see in camera menus, tutorials and editing apps.

24 terms
Aperture

The opening in a lens. A lower f-number means a wider opening and usually a blurrier background.

Shutter speed

How long the sensor is exposed. Faster speeds freeze motion; slower speeds show more movement.

ISO

The camera's sensitivity setting. Higher ISO helps in low light but can add visible noise.

Exposure

The amount of light recorded in an image. It is shaped by aperture, shutter speed and ISO.

Focal length

A lens measurement in millimetres that describes its angle of view, not simply how far it zooms.

Depth of field

The zone in front of and behind the focus point that appears acceptably sharp.

RAW

A flexible image file containing more processing data than a typical JPEG. It needs development.

White balance

A setting that makes neutral objects look neutral under different colour temperatures.

Histogram

A graph showing how image tones are distributed from dark to bright.

AF-C / Servo

Continuous autofocus that keeps adjusting focus while the subject or camera moves.

AF-S / One-Shot

Single autofocus that focuses once when activated; useful for subjects that are still.

F-stop

The aperture number. Each full stop changes the light by a factor of two.

Focal plane

The plane where the sensor records a focused image.

Rule of thirds

A composition guide that places key elements near a 3-by-3 grid's lines or intersections.

Leading lines

Lines in a scene that guide the viewer's eye toward a subject or through the frame.

Bokeh

The character of the out-of-focus areas in an image, not just the amount of blur.

Frame rate (fps)

How many frames are captured each second in video.

180° shutter

A common video guideline: shutter time is about 1 divided by twice the frame rate.

Bitrate

How much data video uses per second. Higher bitrate can preserve more detail but creates larger files.

Codec

The method used to encode and decode video, such as H.264, H.265 or ProRes.

Log profile

A flat-looking recording profile designed to preserve more tonal flexibility for grading.

Rolling shutter

Sensor readout timing that can make fast motion or quick pans appear skewed.

Focus breathing

A slight change in framing as a lens changes focus distance.

Stabilisation

Optical, sensor-based or digital methods that reduce camera shake in photos or video.
